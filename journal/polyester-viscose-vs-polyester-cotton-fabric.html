PV & Blended Fabrics

Polyester Viscose vs Polyester Cotton (TC): Which Fabric Should Bulk Buyers Choose?

By ENZO Textiles · Published 4 November 2026 · 5 min read

Polyester viscose (PV, often called TR) and polyester cotton (TC) are the two most common polyester blends in uniform, workwear and suiting cloth. Both use polyester for strength and easy care. The difference is the second fibre: viscose gives softness and drape, cotton gives a familiar breathable hand and better wet strength. Neither is universally better — the right choice depends on the garment, the climate and how the cloth will be washed.

What each blend is

  • PV / TR — polyester blended with viscose (rayon), a regenerated cellulosic fibre made from wood pulp. Described in trade usage as the smoother, better-draping option (Iyunai Textile).
  • TC — polyester blended with cotton, a natural fibre. Commonly cited for breathability, durability and lower cost in everyday uniforms (Iyunai Textile).

For the full background on PV see our guide to polyester viscose fabric.

The fibre-level difference that drives everything

The clearest measurable difference is moisture regain — how much water a fibre holds at standard conditions. Commercial values from ASTM D1909 put polyester at 0.4%, cotton at 7.0–8.5% depending on the yarn form, and rayon at 11.0%; other published data put viscose at about 13%. A blend's regain is the weighted average of its fibres (NPTEL), which gives these estimates:

Blend (polyester/second fibre)CalculationEstimated regain
65/35 polyester–cotton0.65×0.4 + 0.35×(7.0–8.5)≈2.7–3.2%
65/35 polyester–viscose0.65×0.4 + 0.35×(11–13)≈4.1–4.8%

Estimates of fibre-level behaviour only. Measured comfort depends on yarn count, twist, construction and finishing.

So at the same ratio, a PV blend holds more moisture than a TC blend. Cotton, however, keeps its strength when wet, whereas viscose loses strength when damp and can be dimensionally unstable (Testex; Testex). Those two facts explain most of the practical differences below. For a deeper look at the numbers, read moisture regain explained.

The "typical positioning" row reflects supplier and manufacturer commentary rather than test data. One manufacturer summary puts it this way: polyester cotton tends to favour durability and easy care, while polyester viscose tends to favour comfort and a more natural feel (Fumao Fabric). Another describes poly-cotton as the practical choice for frequent washing in workplace uniforms (Mafatlal). Treat these as starting hypotheses and verify them with your own tests.

How to choose by use

Suiting, trousers and formal wear

PV is the usual starting point because of its drape and surface. Specify the weight and construction carefully — see fabric for formal wear — and test pilling and dimensional change.

School and office uniforms

Both blends are used. Frequent washing makes wet strength and shrinkage the deciding factors, so request wash-shrinkage and pilling results for the exact cloth. See fabric for uniforms for the full specification checklist.

Hot-climate shirting

At the fibre level viscose absorbs more moisture than cotton, but cotton keeps its strength when wet, and construction (open weave, lighter yarns) can matter more than the blend label. Compare actual weights and constructions rather than names; our guide on comparing fabrics side by side shows how.

How to verify the choice before a bulk order

  1. Ask for a full construction: yarn counts, EPI × PPI, weave and GSM.
  2. Request lab results for dimensional change, pilling and colour fastness, citing the standards — see fabric quality tests and ISO standards.
  3. Wash-test a sample under your real care conditions.
  4. Compare cost on a like-for-like GSM and width basis, not on price per metre alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is PV or TC better for uniforms?

Neither wins outright. Supplier commentary favours TC for easy care under heavy washing and PV for softness and drape; the deciding evidence should be wash-shrinkage and pilling test results on the exact fabric.

Is TR the same as PV?

In trade usage TR usually means polyester–rayon, and rayon and viscose belong to the same fibre family, so TR and PV commonly describe the same kind of cloth.

Which is more breathable, PV or TC?

At the fibre level viscose absorbs more moisture than cotton, so a PV blend can have a higher regain than a TC blend of the same ratio. Real-world breathability also depends heavily on construction and weight.

Which blend shrinks more?

Viscose is described as dimensionally less stable than polyester, so PV should be tested for shrinkage. Do not assume either blend is stable without a test under a named standard.

What blend ratio should I order?

For polyester–viscose, 65/35 and 48/52 are commonly used for suiting and uniforms. For polyester–cotton, 65/35 is a widely cited workwear ratio. Choose by end use and test the result.

Conclusion

PV and TC solve different problems. Choose PV when drape and a smooth hand matter and you can control washing; choose TC when wet strength and easy care under repeated laundering come first. Either way, buy on a written construction and test results, not on the blend name.
