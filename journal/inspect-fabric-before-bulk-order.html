How to Inspect Fabric Before Placing a Bulk Order

Cluster: Quality Control · Primary keyword: how to inspect fabric before bulk order

The most valuable inspection happens before the purchase order is placed, not after the fabric ships. Here's the sequence that catches problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Step 1: Review the pre-production sample against your specification

Check construction (EPI/PPI — see our density-reading guide), GSM, and composition against what was quoted. This should be from the actual production run, not a generic swatch — always confirm which you've been sent.

Step 2: Check for weaving defects

See our full list in common greige fabric defects — missing ends, broken picks, slubs, reed marks, weft bars. These are cheapest to catch before dyeing and finishing cost is added.

Step 3: Confirm width and GSM tolerance in writing

Not just the target figures — the acceptable range. See why consistency matters more than averages for why a tight average with wide variation is a worse outcome than a slightly off-target but consistent lot.

Step 4: Agree the inspection standard for the finished lot

The four-point system is the industry standard — see our full fabric inspection checklist for the scoring method and a worked example.

Step 5: Confirm the remedy if the lot fails

Replacement, discount, or rejection — decided now, in writing, is a commercial term. Decided after the fact, it's a negotiation with much less leverage on your side.

The five things to have in writing before production starts: inspection standard and acceptable score, GSM tolerance and basis (greige or finished), width tolerance, shade approval method, and remedy for a failed lot. Every one of these is cheap to agree up front and expensive to argue about after shipment.

Frequently asked questions

Should I inspect every roll in a bulk order?

Full inspection is standard for high-value or first-time orders. Otherwise a representative sample — commonly around 10% of rolls, selected across the lot — is normal practice.

What if I don't have inspection equipment?

A pick glass and a basic scale cover the essentials. Third-party inspection services are also an option for buyers who can't inspect in person, especially for international orders.
