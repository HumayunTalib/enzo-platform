Quality Control · BOFU

A Fabric Inspection Checklist for Bulk Orders

Cluster: Quality Control · Primary keyword: fabric inspection checklist

A defect found on the inspection table costs you a roll. The same defect found after cutting costs you the cut parts, the labour already spent, and the delivery date.

Inspect at three points, not one

  1. Pre-production sample — from the actual production run, not the sales swatch.
  2. In-line, during production — catches a drifting loom or dye-lot problem while there's time to correct it.
  3. Pre-shipment — the final gate, before the lot leaves the supplier.

The four-point system

The most widely used fabric inspection standard in apparel textiles. Every defect is assigned penalty points by its length, normalised to a fixed area.

Defect sizePenalty points
Up to 3 inches1
Over 3 up to 6 inches2
Over 6 up to 9 inches3
Over 9 inches4
Holes up to 1 inch2
Holes over 1 inch4

No single linear yard can be penalised more than 4 points, however many defects it contains.

Points per 100 sq yd = ( Total penalty points × 3600 ) ÷ ( Fabric length in yards × Fabric width in inches )

Worked example: a 120-yard roll at 58 inches wide with 38 penalty points:

( 38 × 3600 ) ÷ ( 120 × 58 ) = 136,800 ÷ 6,960 = 19.7 points per 100 sq yd

Acceptance thresholds are contractual, not universal — many apparel buyers work to 20–40 points per 100 sq yd depending on end use.

Dimensional and construction checks

  • Width — measure at multiple points, not once.
  • GSM — cut with a GSM cutter and weigh at more than one point.
  • Ends and picks per inch — compare against the contracted construction with a pick glass.

Common defects on woven suiting

DefectTypical cause
SlubsThick places in the yarn
Warp streaksTension variation or mixed yarn lots
Weft barsLoom stoppage, pick density change
Shade variationDye lot inconsistency

Agree these in writing before production

  1. Inspection standard and acceptable points per 100 sq yd
  2. GSM tolerance, and whether measured greige or finished
  3. Width tolerance
  4. Shade approval method and light source
  5. Remedy if a lot fails

Frequently asked questions

What is an acceptable four-point score?

There's no universal figure — it's set by contract, often 20–40 points per 100 sq yd. Agree the threshold before production.

Should I inspect 100% of a lot?

Full inspection is standard for high-value or problem-history lots. Otherwise a meaningful sample, often around 10% of rolls, is normal practice.
