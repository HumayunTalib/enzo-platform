Greige Fabric vs Finished Fabric: What Actually Changes

Cluster: Greige Fabric · Primary keyword: greige vs finished fabric

Colour is the obvious difference between greige and finished fabric. It isn't the only one — and the ones buyers miss are the ones that cause disputes on a purchase order.

What processing actually does

Between the loom and the finished roll, fabric typically passes through some combination of: scouring (removing natural oils, dirt, and sizing chemicals), bleaching, dyeing or printing, and mechanical or chemical finishing (calendering, softening, water-repellent treatment, and so on, depending on end use).

Four things that change, not just colour

PropertyWhat typically happens
Weight (GSM)Can increase or decrease depending on the finish — shrinkage during wet processing often increases GSM per unit area even as total fabric mass is roughly conserved
WidthFabric commonly shrinks in width during wet processing; finished width is narrower than loom width
HandSizing chemicals are removed and softening finishes applied — finished fabric almost always feels noticeably different from greige
Dimensional stabilityFinishing processes (like sanforizing) are specifically used to control residual shrinkage in the finished garment
The practical consequence: a GSM or width figure quoted at the greige stage will not match the finished roll. Always confirm which basis a specification refers to — see our note on this in what GSM actually measures.

Why this matters for costing and ordering

If you're buying greige and arranging your own dyeing and finishing, budget for the shrinkage and hand change separately — the construction (reed, pick, yarn) carries through unchanged, but the finished piece will not weigh, measure, or feel like the greige sample you approved.

Why this matters for quality control

Inspection should happen at both stages where possible: greige inspection catches weaving defects before the cost of dyeing and finishing is added on top of them; finished-stage inspection catches shade, hand, and dimensional issues introduced during processing. See our fabric inspection checklist for the full standard.

Frequently asked questions

Does finishing change the fabric's construction?

No — reed, pick, and weave pattern are fixed at weaving. Finishing changes appearance, hand, weight and dimensions, not the underlying interlacing.

Why does fabric shrink in width during finishing?

Wet processing relaxes tension held in the fabric from weaving, and the cloth naturally draws in — this is why finishing lines often include a controlled stretching (tentering) step to manage final width.
