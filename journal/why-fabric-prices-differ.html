Why Two Similar-Looking Fabrics Can Have Very Different Prices

Cluster: Costing & Pricing · Primary keyword: why fabric prices differ

Two fabrics on the same shelf, similar colour, similar hand, priced 50% apart. Nothing about that gap is arbitrary — it's almost always explained by one or more of a small set of measurable variables.

1. Yarn fineness

A finer yarn (higher count number) costs more to spin and typically produces a more refined hand — see our explainer on what yarn count means. Two fabrics at the same weight can use very different yarn fineness, with the finer one costing meaningfully more per metre.

2. Construction density

Higher ends and picks per inch mean more yarn packed into the same area of fabric — more material, more loom time, higher cost. See our guide to reed and pick for how to check this yourself with a pick glass.

3. Fibre content

Natural fibres and specialty synthetics typically cost more per unit weight than standard polyester or viscose blends. A fabric's composition line on the specification sheet is a direct cost driver.

4. Finishing treatment

Additional finishing steps — softening, water-repellent treatment, specialty calendering — all add cost. See our piece on how finishing affects price and feel.

5. Dye complexity

Some shades require multiple dye passes or more expensive dye classes to achieve, especially very deep or very specific colours, adding processing cost beyond the base fabric.

6. Brand and distribution markup

This is the variable least connected to the physical fabric — how many intermediaries the fabric passed through before reaching the shelf. See our piece on fabric supplier vs fabric trader for how this layer adds cost without changing the product.

How to check for yourself: ask for the construction (reed × pick), yarn count, and composition of both fabrics. If those numbers are genuinely close and the price gap is still large, the difference is more likely in distribution markup than in the fabric itself.

What price gap is "normal"

There's no universal answer — but a 10–20% gap between comparable constructions from different suppliers is unremarkable; a 50%+ gap on fabrics with genuinely matching specifications is worth a direct question rather than an assumption in either direction.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher price always mean better quality?

Not automatically — see our full piece on quality vs price. Price should track measurable specification differences, not just the fabric's name or where it's sold.
