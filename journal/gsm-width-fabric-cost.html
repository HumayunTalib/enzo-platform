GSM and Width: How They Change Fabric Cost Per Metre

Cluster: Costing & Pricing · Primary keyword: GSM and fabric cost

GSM and width both affect cost, but through different mechanisms — one changes how much material is in each metre, the other changes how many metres you actually need. Confusing the two leads to comparing quotes that aren't actually comparable.

GSM drives material cost per metre

Higher GSM means more yarn packed into each square metre of fabric — directly increasing the warp and weft weight, and therefore the yarn cost, in the costing formula covered in how fabric is priced. All else equal, a heavier construction costs more per metre to produce.

Width drives how many metres you need

A wider fabric covers more area per linear metre, which means fewer metres are needed to produce the same garment or order quantity. A fabric quoted at a higher rate per metre but a wider finished width can be the cheaper option once you calculate total cost per garment rather than cost per metre.

Cost per garment ≈ (Rate per metre) × (Marker consumption at that width)

Marker consumption — how much fabric a garment pattern actually uses — changes with width, so this figure has to be recalculated for each width being compared, not assumed constant.

The comparison mistake to avoid: comparing two quotes purely on rate-per-metre when they're at different widths or GSM. Normalise both to cost-per-garment or cost-per-square-metre before deciding which is actually cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

Does higher GSM always mean higher cost?

All else equal, yes — more yarn per square metre means more material cost. But construction density (reed/pick) and yarn fineness also drive GSM, so two fabrics at the same weight can still cost differently.

Should I always choose the wider fabric?

Only if the total cost-per-garment calculation favours it — a wider fabric at a disproportionately higher rate isn't automatically the better deal. Run the numbers for your specific pattern.
