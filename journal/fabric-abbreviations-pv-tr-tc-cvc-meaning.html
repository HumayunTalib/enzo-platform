Terminology

PV, TR, TC, CVC: What Fabric Abbreviations Mean and How to Write Them in an Order

By ENZO Textiles · Published 17 November 2026 · 4 min read

Fabric specifications are full of two- and three-letter codes: PV, TR, TC, CVC, PC. They are trade shorthand for blends, and they are not governed by one international standard. The same letters can mean slightly different things in different markets, which is how buyers and mills end up with the wrong cloth. This guide explains the common meanings, where they conflict, and how to write a composition that cannot be misread.

The common abbreviations

CodeUsually meansTypical ratios mentioned
PVPolyester blended with viscose65/35, 55/45, 48/52 (polyester/viscose)
TRPolyester blended with rayon (viscose), so usually the same cloth family as PVSame as PV
TC (also T/C)Polyester-rich polyester–cottonCommonly 65/35 (polyester/cotton)
CVC“Chief value cotton”: cotton-rich cotton–polyesterOften 60/40 up to 80/20 (cotton/polyester)
PCPolyester–cotton; usage variesOften 65/35, but some markets use it for cotton-rich cloth

Sources agree on the broad pattern: TR describes polyester–rayon fabric and TC polyester–cotton (Iyunai Textile), while CVC stands for chief value cotton, meaning cotton is the main fibre in the blend, in contrast to TC where polyester dominates (Iyunai Textile). Ratios commonly quoted for CVC are 60/40, 65/35 and 80/20 cotton to polyester (Textile Learner).

Where the meanings conflict

These codes are conventions, so you will meet exceptions:

  • CVC thresholds differ. Industry commentary describes CVC as cotton above 50% and often around 60/40 (Graphics Pro), but ranges up to 80% cotton and beyond are also called CVC. “CVC” alone does not tell you the ratio.
  • The order of the names. Many suppliers name the larger fibre first, so “polyester–cotton” is polyester-rich and “cotton–polyester” is cotton-rich (Tex Suppliers). This is a habit, not a rule, and it is easy to reverse by mistake.
  • “PC” is ambiguous. Some sellers use it for polyester-rich cloth, others for cotton-rich blends.
  • Regional trade names. The “T” in TC refers to a polyester trade name, spelled differently across sources, which is why buyers sometimes see “Terylene cotton” and sometimes “Tetron cotton”.

Why it matters for your order

The fibre split changes how the cloth behaves. At the fibre level, polyester absorbs almost no moisture, cotton absorbs far more and viscose more again (see moisture regain explained). A cotton-rich CVC cloth and a polyester-rich TC cloth can differ in comfort, shrinkage risk and cost even though both are “poly-cotton”. For the viscose side, see the PV fabric guide, and for how the two cellulosic partners compare, PV vs TC.

How to write composition so it cannot be misread

  1. Use full fibre names and percentages. Write “65% polyester / 35% viscose”, not “65/35 PV”.
  2. State the basis. Percentages are by fibre weight. Say so, and give the tolerance you accept (for example ±3 points).
  3. Keep the order consistent. List the fibres from highest to lowest percentage.
  4. Say where the blend is. Specify whether warp and weft yarns are both blended, or whether each system is a different fibre.
  5. Add the rest of the construction. Composition is one line of a specification. Yarn count, ends × picks, weave and weight complete it; see how to read a fabric specification sheet.
  6. Ask for proof when it matters. A laboratory fibre analysis is the reliable check on a stated composition.

Frequently asked questions

What does CVC stand for in fabric?

Chief value cotton: a cotton–polyester blend in which cotton is the main fibre, commonly 60/40 up to 80/20 cotton to polyester.

What does TC fabric mean?

Polyester–cotton in which polyester is the larger share, most often quoted as 65% polyester and 35% cotton.

What does TR fabric mean?

Polyester–rayon. Because rayon and viscose belong to the same fibre family, TR and PV usually describe the same type of cloth.

Is PV the same as TR?

Usually yes in trade usage, but confirm the exact composition in writing.

What is the difference between TC and CVC?

TC is polyester-dominant; CVC is cotton-dominant. The first tends to favour easy care and durability, the second a cotton-like hand. Confirm percentages, because usage varies.

Conclusion

Treat PV, TR, TC and CVC as shorthand to be expanded, never as a specification on their own. Write the full fibre names and percentages and the rest of the order follows more reliably.
