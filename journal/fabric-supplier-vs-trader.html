Fabric Supplier vs Fabric Trader: What's the Difference?

Cluster: Supplier & Manufacturer · Primary keyword: fabric supplier vs trader

"Fabric supplier" gets used loosely to describe three genuinely different businesses — and the difference matters more than the shared label suggests.

The three layers

TypeControls production?What they optimise forTypical MOQ
Manufacturer (mill)YesLoom utilisation, repeat volumeHigher — thousands of metres
Trader / stockistNoMargin per transactionLower — can go to hundreds of metres
Agent / export houseNoCommission on placed volumeVaries by the mills they represent

Why the distinction matters

A manufacturer can commit to construction changes, tolerances, and consistency across reorders because they control the process directly. A trader is reselling whatever they can source, which means lot-to-lot consistency depends on their own supplier relationships — not something they fully control either. Neither is wrong for every situation, but they solve different problems.

The one-question test: ask what construction they're running right now, and where it's woven. A manufacturer answers specifically. A trader or agent typically answers in terms of what's currently available rather than what they can produce to order.

When each makes sense

  • Manufacturer — best for repeat orders of a fixed construction at volume, where consistency across reorders matters most.
  • Trader — best for small or highly varied orders below a manufacturer's MOQ, or when speed of availability from stock matters more than customisation.
  • Agent — useful when you want one point of contact across multiple mills, at the cost of an added commission layer.

How this affects price

Each layer between you and the loom typically adds margin without changing the physical fabric — see our note on this in why two similar-looking fabrics can cost differently.

Frequently asked questions

Is buying direct from a manufacturer always cheaper?

Usually, for volume that meets their MOQ — but a trader can be more cost-effective for small or highly varied orders where a manufacturer's minimum doesn't apply.

How do I ask a supplier which type they are without being rude?

Ask about the construction of something they're running now, and where it's woven — a direct, practical question that naturally reveals the answer without confrontation.
