How to Choose a Fabric Supplier: A Commercial Buyer's Checklist

Cluster: Supplier & Manufacturer · Primary keyword: how to choose a fabric supplier

You have three quotations for the same construction and the prices differ by twenty percent. Nothing in the quotes explains why. The decision is rarely about the quoted rate — it's about whether the fabric that arrives in month six matches the sample you approved in month one.

First, establish who you're actually buying from

TypeControls production?Risk to you
Mill / manufacturerYesMay decline very small orders
Trader / stockistNoLot-to-lot variation between sources
Sourcing agentNoOpaque supplier substitution

Ask for the construction of a fabric they're running right now, and where it's woven. A manufacturer answers immediately and specifically.

Verify capability before you discuss price

Construction capability

Give the supplier your target construction in full — warp count, weft count, ends per inch, picks per inch, finished width, target GSM — and ask whether they can run it. A supplier who can will engage with the numbers, possibly pushing back on one for a technical reason. That pushback is a good sign.

Consistency across repeat orders

Ask directly: what GSM tolerance do you work to, and is it stated in the contract? What width tolerance between lots? How do you handle shade continuity between dye lots?

Quality control process

Ask what inspection standard they run against — see our fabric inspection checklist for what to specify.

Eight questions that separate suppliers from resellers

  1. Where is this woven — specifically?
  2. What is your MOQ, per construction and per shade?
  3. What GSM and width tolerances do you contract to?
  4. What is your realistic lead time, including dyeing?
  5. What inspection standard do you run, and will you share the report?
  6. Can I have a pre-production sample from the actual run?
  7. What happens commercially if a lot fails inspection?
  8. Who is my point of contact after the order is placed?
On price claims: treat "cheapest in the market" the same way you'd treat any unevidenced claim. A supplier who competes only on rate has to recover it somewhere — usually in yarn quality, tolerance, or the second order.

Frequently asked questions

Should I always buy direct from a mill?

Not always. Mills usually have higher minimums. Direct supply makes most sense when ordering the same construction repeatedly at volume.

What is a reasonable MOQ for woven fabric?

Commonly thousands to tens of thousands of metres per construction. ENZO's wholesale MOQ is 10,000 m.
