Retail Education

The Specs Most Unstitched Suits Don't Print

By ENZO Textiles · Published 27 November 2026 · 7 min read

Walk through any large retailer’s men’s unstitched range, in store or online, and you will see tier names, a suit length, a colour and perhaps one functional label such as “Easy Iron” or “All Season”. You will rarely see the numbers that decide how the cloth will feel, wear and stitch. This guide explains how unstitched suits are usually listed, which specifications actually decide quality, and how a wholesaler or shop can ask for them.

How unstitched suits are usually listed

Retail listings in Pakistan follow a fairly consistent pattern. A few publicly visible examples, viewed in November 2026:

  • J. organises men’s unstitched by tiers such as Platinum Class, Gold Class and Silver Class, alongside Latha and Boski collections. Its filters offer fabric type (Blended, Cotton, Polyester), season (All Seasons, Summer) and one size, “4.5 Meters”. Menu banners use labels such as “Easy Iron” and “Summer Cool” (J. men’s unstitched).
  • Edenrobe lists men’s unstitched articles under names such as Majestic, Prestige, Gold, Platinum, Empire and Elite. The fabric filter shows “Blended”, the season filter “All Season”, and the page copy mentions cotton, wash & wear, khaddar, blended and boski materials in general terms (Edenrobe men unstitched).
  • Marketplace listings follow the same pattern. One Daraz listing titled as a Gul Ahmed men’s unstitched suit describes “4.25 Meter Premium Wash & Wear Fabric for All-Season Elegance” (Daraz listing). Marketplace listings are posted by sellers and may not be written by the brand itself.

On the listing pages we reviewed, the product tiles did not state GSM, construction, yarn count, width or blend percentages. That is not a criticism of any brand. Retail listings are written for a shopper choosing a colour and a price, not for a buyer comparing cloth. Brands may hold detailed specifications internally, and some may give them on request.

Why listings look like this

A shopper buying one suit wants three things answered quickly: is it the right colour, is there enough cloth for my size, and will it be easy to look after? A tier name, a length and one care label answer those questions. For the cut length itself, one retailer’s guide says a suit needs “about 4.5 to 5.5 metres” depending on height and design, and advises checking with a tailor (Alkaram Studio fabric guide). Another seller’s guide gives suit pieces of 4 to 4.5 m in double width (Sheikh Gulzar Fabrics). Our shalwar kameez fabric quantity guide covers the arithmetic.

A wholesaler or shop buying hundreds of suits has different questions. Will every suit in the lot look and feel the same? Will it hold up after washing? Is a higher price buying a better cloth or a better name? Those questions need numbers.

The numbers that decide quality

Reed × pick (construction)Threads per inch in warp and weft: how densely the cloth is wovenReed and pick
GSMWeight per square metre: body, warmth and seasonWhat is GSM
BlendFibre percentages: crease recovery, comfort, how the cloth takes dyePV, TR, TC, CVC

No single number decides quality. A high construction with coarse yarn can be heavy and stiff; a fine yarn at low density can feel thin. Weight follows from construction and yarn count together, as our yarn count and fabric weight guide shows. The blend decides how the cloth behaves after washing, which is the whole point of a wash and wear fabric. Our wash and wear fabric guide explains why.

Two further points are often missed. First, a functional label such as “Easy Iron” describes a result, not a method, so it tells you nothing about blend or finish. Second, tier names are internal to each brand. A “Gold” at one retailer and a “Gold” at another have no fixed relationship to each other.

Comparing two suits side by side

If you cannot get written specifications, you can still compare two suit pieces fairly:

  1. Weigh and measure. Measure length and width, weigh the piece, and work out an approximate GSM: weight in grams divided by area in square metres. Our GSM, width and cost guide shows how weight and width feed into price per suit.
  2. Count threads. Use a pick glass to count ends and picks per inch on each.
  3. Crease test. Crush a corner of each for the same time and compare recovery.
  4. Check the blend. A fibre content label or a laboratory test settles the blend; guessing by feel is unreliable.
  5. Wash one sample. Measure before and after to check shrinkage, and look for shade change.

Our general method for comparing fabrics and the article on fabric quality vs price go further.

How to ask for the numbers

When you buy for resale, ask your supplier for a written specification with each quotation. A simple request is enough:

  • Construction (reed × pick), stated as greige or finished.
  • Blend as percentages.
  • Warp and weft yarn counts.
  • GSM and usable width, each with a tolerance.
  • Shade card, and how bulk shade will be checked.
  • Metres per piece, and how pieces are inspected.

A supplier who can answer these in writing is giving you something you can check on delivery. Our woven fabric specification sheet template sets the fields out on one page, and the yarn specs before a bulk order article explains the yarn details to fix in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Why don’t unstitched suit listings show GSM?

Retail listings are written for shoppers choosing colour, length and price. Detailed specifications are usually held by the brand or the mill rather than printed on the listing.

Does “Platinum” or “Gold” mean a heavier or better fabric?

Tier names are set by each brand and are not industry grades. Compare the specifications, not the names.

How many metres is a men’s unstitched suit?

Listings commonly show 4 to 4.5 m, and one retailer’s guide suggests 4.5 to 5.5 m depending on height and design. Width also matters, so check with your tailor.

What is the single most useful number to ask for?

There isn’t one. Ask for construction, GSM and blend together, because each changes what the others mean.

Can I estimate GSM from a suit piece?

Yes. Divide the piece’s weight in grams by its area in square metres. It is an approximation, but useful for comparing two pieces.

Conclusion

Retail listings tell a shopper what a suit is for. They rarely tell a trade buyer what it is made of. When you buy in quantity, ask for construction, GSM, blend, yarn count and width in writing, and check them on delivery.
