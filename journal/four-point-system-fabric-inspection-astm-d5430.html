The 4-Point System for Fabric Inspection (ASTM D5430): How Points Are Counted and Graded

By ENZO Textiles · Published 20 November 2026 · 4 min read

The 4-point system is the most widely used way to grade rolls of woven fabric from a visual inspection. Each visible defect earns 1 to 4 penalty points according to its size; the points are totalled and converted to a score per 100 square yards (or 100 square metres), and the roll is accepted, downgraded or rejected against a limit that buyer and seller have agreed. It is described in ASTM D5430 (V-Trust).

How defects are scored

Points depend on the length of the defect, measured in either direction (warp or weft). A single defect can never receive more than 4 points, and only major defects are penalised (V-Trust).

3 in or lessup to 75 mm1

Over 6 in to 9 in150 mm to 230 mm3
Over 9 inover 230 mm4

Source: ASTM D5430 table as reproduced by V-Trust. Some buyer specifications add their own rules, such as a cap on points per linear yard, so read the specification that applies to your order.

The formulas

Points are normalised to a standard area so that rolls of different lengths and widths can be compared. The calculation in imperial units is:

Points per 100 sq yd = (total points × 36 × 100) / (fabric width in inches × length inspected in yards)

A metric version uses width in millimetres (DXLG quality assurance specification):

Points per 100 sq m = (total points × 100,000) / (length inspected in metres × width in mm)

Use the cuttable (usable) width, not the full selvedge-to-selvedge width, if your specification says so; see how fabric width affects garment consumption.

Worked examples

Example 1: imperial

A published example inspects a 120-yard roll, 45 inches wide, and finds 22 points in total (V-Trust):

(22 × 36 × 100) / (45 × 120) = 14.66 points per 100 sq yd

Example 2: metric (illustrative)

A 100 m roll, 1,500 mm wide, shows 6 defects up to 75 mm (6 points), 3 defects of 75–150 mm (6 points), 1 defect of 150–230 mm (3 points) and 1 defect over 230 mm (4 points). Total: 19 points.

(19 × 100,000) / (100 × 1,500) = 12.67 points per 100 sq m

Check: the roll is 150 sq m, so 19 points over 150 sq m is 12.67 points per 100 sq m.

Setting an acceptance limit

The system produces a score, not a verdict. The pass limit is a commercial decision. One inspection firm notes that some factories treat up to 40 points per 100 sq yd as acceptable, while buyers and brands set their own limits (V-Trust). Because 100 sq yd is about 83.6 sq m, 40 points per 100 sq yd is roughly 48 points per 100 sq m, and the metric figure in a published buyer specification matches that conversion (DXLG).

Write the limit into the order:

  1. State the standard (ASTM D5430 4-point system) and the unit (per 100 sq yd or per 100 sq m).
  2. Set a maximum score per roll and, if you wish, a maximum average for the lot.
  3. Define how many rolls are inspected, and whether inspection is at the mill, before shipment or on arrival.
  4. Say what happens to failed rolls: replacement, credit, or acceptance as seconds.

What the 4-point system does not cover

It grades visible defects only. It does not measure weight, shrinkage, strength or colour fastness; those need laboratory tests such as those in fabric quality tests for bulk buyers. It also does not replace a construction check, covered in how to calculate fabric GSM. Frequently asked questions

What is the 4-point system in fabric inspection?

A method that gives each defect 1–4 penalty points by size and converts the total to points per 100 square yards or metres, so rolls can be graded consistently.

How many points is acceptable?

There is no universal limit. Some factories treat up to 40 points per 100 sq yd as acceptable, but buyers set their own limits; agree the number in the order.

How do you calculate points per 100 square yards?

Multiply total points by 36 and by 100, then divide by fabric width in inches times length inspected in yards.

Can one defect get more than 4 points?

No. A single defect is capped at 4 points.

Does the 4-point system work for greige and finished fabric?

Yes. It can be applied to any woven fabric, grey or finished, as long as the criteria are agreed in advance.

Conclusion

Learn the four size bands, apply the formula to the cuttable width, and agree the limit before production. A score on a shared scale replaces an argument about “a few defects”.
