Why the Same Weight Feels Different

Cluster: Terminology · Primary keyword: what is GSM fabric

Two fabrics can carry the exact same weight on a scale and behave nothing alike on the body. Weight — measured in GSM, grams per square metre — tells you how heavy a cloth is. It tells you almost nothing about how it moves.

What GSM actually measures

GSM is a straightforward physical measurement: cut a fixed area of fabric, weigh it, and express the result as grams per square metre. It is useful and comparable across fabrics — but it is only one variable, and buyers frequently ask it to do a job it can't: predict how a fabric will drape, feel, or wear.

Drape is a separate variable

Drape comes from how the yarn is spun and twisted, not from how much of it there is per square metre. A crepe weave with a tight twist stands slightly away from the body no matter how light it's woven — that's what gives a micro-crepe construction its dry, springy hand at a comparatively low GSM. A fabric engineered for fluid fall uses finer filament and a looser construction to fall close and move with light, even at a heavier GSM.

The practical upshot: when you're choosing fabric, ask about drape separately from weight. "Heavy" doesn't mean "structured," and "light" doesn't mean "flimsy" — they're different questions with different answers.

How to use GSM correctly when comparing fabrics

  • Use it to compare fabrics of similar construction. GSM is most meaningful when comparing two versions of a similar weave, not a crepe against a worsted.
  • Confirm whether it's greige or finished. Dyeing and finishing change weight — a specification sheet should say which basis it's quoting.
  • Pair it with a hand-feel check. Let the fabric hang for a moment before you ask the weight number. If it holds a shape rather than collapsing, weight alone won't tell you that.

Frequently asked questions

Does a higher GSM mean better quality?

No. GSM measures weight, not quality. A 150 GSM summer fabric and a 250 GSM winter fabric can both be excellent — they're built for different conditions.

Is GSM measured before or after dyeing?

It can be either — always confirm which basis a specification refers to, since finishing changes fabric weight.

Can two fabrics have the same GSM but different weight in the hand?

Yes. Fibre density and yarn twist both affect perceived weight independently of GSM.
