How Much Fabric Does a Shalwar Kameez Actually Require?

Cluster: Retail Education · Primary keyword: how much fabric for shalwar kameez

Buying short on fabric is one of the most common — and most avoidable — mistakes in ordering unstitched cloth. Here's the actual arithmetic.

The standard figure

At the common 56–58 inch fabric width, a full men's shalwar kameez requires 4 metres. This covers the kameez (the tunic) and the shalwar (the trousers) cut from the same piece.

Fabric widthTypical requirement
56–58 inches (standard)4 m
44–45 inches (narrower)5 m [confirm]

What pushes the figure up

  • Larger sizes. Taller or larger-framed measurements need proportionally more length.
  • Directional patterns or nap. A fabric with a one-way print, stripe, or nap restricts how pattern pieces can be laid out on the cloth, increasing wastage.
  • Longer kameez styles. Some regional and formal styles run longer than the standard cut.
  • Narrower fabric. As shown above, narrower width directly increases the metreage needed — always confirm width before calculating quantity, not just relying on a flat "4 metres" rule of thumb.
The safe-buying rule: when in doubt, round up rather than down. A small offcut is a minor waste; a length that's just short of what your tailor needs means either an awkward compromise on the cut or a second trip to match the dye lot — which isn't always possible.

Buying for multiple garments

If you're buying fabric for more than one person from the same piece — common for family orders — calculate each garment's requirement separately based on that person's size, then sum them, rather than assuming a flat per-person average. See our full fabric-choosing guide for what else to check before you commit to a length.

Frequently asked questions

Is 4 metres always enough?

For a standard-size men's shalwar kameez at 56–58 inch width, yes in most cases — but confirm with your tailor for larger sizes or patterned fabric.

What happens if I buy slightly too little?

Your tailor may need to compromise on the cut, or you'll need to source a matching offcut — which isn't guaranteed to match the dye lot exactly. It's safer to round up.
