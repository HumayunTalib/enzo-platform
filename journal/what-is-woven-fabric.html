What Is Woven Fabric? Construction, Density and Why It Matters

Cluster: Woven Fabric · Primary keyword: what is woven fabric

Woven fabric is made by interlacing two sets of yarn at right angles on a loom: warp, running the length of the piece, and weft, inserted across it pick by pick. That mechanical interlacing — not the fibre, not the colour — is what defines "woven" as a category.

How weaving actually works

Warp yarns are set up on the loom under tension, then the weft yarn is inserted over and under the warp in a repeating pattern, with each pass beaten into place by the reed. The pattern of over-under interlacing determines the weave type — plain, twill, satin, and their variations all come from different interlacing sequences using the same basic mechanism. See our companion piece on warp vs weft for how the two yarn systems differ functionally.

Woven vs knit — the fundamental difference

Knit fabric is built from a single continuous yarn looped through itself repeatedly (like knitting by hand, mechanised). Woven fabric interlaces two separate yarn systems with no loops. This is why woven fabric generally has less inherent stretch than knit, holds a crease and a tailored line better, and is the standard choice for structured garments like suiting — while knit is chosen for garments that need to move with the body.

Reading a woven fabric's density

Density is expressed as ends per inch (warp) and picks per inch (weft) — see our full explainer on reed and pick. Higher numbers in both directions generally mean a tighter, heavier, more durable fabric, though the two numbers don't have to move together.

Why construction matters more than the fabric's trade name: "suiting," "boski," "twill" are all category names. Reed, pick, yarn count and GSM are the actual specification — two fabrics with the same name can be built completely differently.

Frequently asked questions

Is woven fabric always more durable than knit?

Not universally — durability depends on yarn, construction density, and finish in both categories. Woven fabric does typically hold a tailored shape better, which is the main reason it's preferred for structured suiting.

What is fabric density?

The combined ends-per-inch and picks-per-inch count — a direct measure of how tightly the warp and weft yarns are packed into the fabric.
