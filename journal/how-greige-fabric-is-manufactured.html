How Greige Fabric Is Manufactured, Step by Step

Cluster: Greige Fabric · Primary keyword: how greige fabric is manufactured

Before a single pick is woven, most of a fabric's eventual quality has already been decided — in the yarn, and in how the loom is set up. Here's the sequence, step by step.

1. Yarn preparation

Warp and weft yarn arrive from spinning (or are spun in-house) and are wound onto the packages the loom needs — cones for weft insertion, beams for warp. Yarn quality here (evenness, twist, strength) sets a ceiling on the fabric quality that follows; no amount of careful weaving fixes bad yarn. See our note on what yarn count means for the specification this stage is built around.

2. Warping

Individual warp yarns are wound together onto a warp beam in the exact count and order the construction requires — this is where the "ends per inch" figure is physically set up before weaving even starts.

3. Sizing

Warp yarn is coated with a sizing agent (often a starch-based compound) to bind the fibres and reduce friction damage as the yarn is repeatedly abraded by the loom during weaving. This sizing is later removed in scouring, which is why greige fabric feels stiffer than the finished cloth.

4. Weaving

The sized warp beam is loaded onto the loom, and weft is inserted pick by pick — via shuttle, rapier, or air/water jet depending on the loom type — building the fabric to its target reed and pick count. This is where the construction figure quoted on the specification sheet (e.g. "108 × 76") is physically realised.

5. Inspection and winding

Woven fabric is inspected on the loom or immediately after — checking for missing ends, broken picks, and other weaving defects — then wound into rolls. See our guide to common greige fabric defects for what inspectors are actually looking for at this stage.

Why this sequence matters to a buyer: a quality problem traced back to yarn preparation or warping is much harder (and more expensive) to fix downstream than one caught at greige inspection. This is the argument for in-line checks, not just a final pre-shipment inspection — see our full inspection checklist.

What happens after greige

Greige fabric then moves to scouring, dyeing or printing, and finishing — see greige vs finished fabric for what changes at that stage.

Frequently asked questions

What is sizing in fabric manufacturing?

A protective coating (often starch-based) applied to warp yarn before weaving, to reduce friction damage from the loom. It's removed during scouring, before dyeing.

Can weaving defects be fixed after the fact?

Some can be repaired by hand (broken picks, small flaws); structural issues like consistent width or density drift generally can't be corrected after weaving and result in the roll being downgraded or rejected.
