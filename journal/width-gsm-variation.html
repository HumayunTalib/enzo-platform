Width and GSM Variation: Why Consistency Matters More Than Averages

Cluster: Quality Control · Primary keyword: fabric width variation

A lot that averages exactly the quoted GSM can still cause real problems downstream if individual rolls swing far above and below that average. Averages hide variation — and variation is what actually breaks a production run.

Why variation matters more than the average

Garment cutting, fabric consumption calculations, and colour matching are all done against a specific expected GSM and width. If roll-to-roll variation is wide, some rolls will consume more fabric than planned, some garments will feel or drape slightly differently from others in the same batch, and consumption estimates calculated from the average will be systematically wrong for a portion of the lot.

Setting a sensible tolerance

Rather than accepting "GSM: 200" as a complete specification, a tolerance should be attached — for example, ±5%. This should be agreed in writing before production and checked at inspection by weighing samples from multiple points across the lot, not just one roll. See our fabric inspection checklist for how this fits into a full QC process.

Acceptable range = Target GSM × (1 ± Tolerance) Example: 200 GSM × (1 ± 5%) = 190 – 210 GSM acceptable range

Width variation and its downstream effect

Width variation has a direct consumption impact — see how GSM and width change cost per metre. A roll that measures narrower than specified yields fewer usable garment pieces per metre than planned, effectively raising the real cost of that roll above the quoted rate.

Where variation typically comes from

  • Inconsistent loom tension control during weaving
  • Yarn lot changes mid-production
  • Uneven finishing tension (tentering) causing width drift
  • Sampling only one point on a roll rather than checking across its length
The practical takeaway: a tight average with wide variation is a worse commercial outcome than a slightly off-target average with tight variation — the second is at least predictable and can be planned around.

Frequently asked questions

What's a reasonable GSM tolerance?

There's no universal figure — it depends on end use and price point — but it should always be a specific agreed percentage in writing, not left unstated.

How is variation actually measured across a lot?

By sampling multiple rolls and multiple points within each roll, rather than relying on a single spot check — see our fuller inspection checklist for a full sampling approach.
