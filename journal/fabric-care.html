Making Finished Suiting Last: A Fabric Care Guide

Cluster: Applications · Primary keyword: fabric care guide

Most suiting fabric fails early not from wear, but from care. A few habits make the real difference over a garment's life.

Four habits worth keeping

Hang, don't fold, between wears. A wooden or padded hanger at the shoulder line prevents the crease lines that folding sets into the fibre over time.

Air before you clean. A day on a hanger in open air resolves most odour and light creasing without a trip to the dry cleaner — over-cleaning is harder on fabric than a day's wear.

Steam over iron where possible. Direct heat and pressure from an iron can flatten a weave's texture permanently; steam relaxes the fibre without crushing it.

Confirm the shade before every cut. Dye lots vary slightly batch to batch — this is why every ENZO shade is checked against the physical swatch at the appointment, not from a screen.

A garment that's hung correctly and steamed rather than ironed will consistently outlast one that isn't — regardless of what the fabric cost.

Frequently asked questions

How often should suiting fabric be dry-cleaned?

Less often than most people assume — airing between wears resolves most of what a dry clean would fix, and over-cleaning stresses the fibre.

Why does my fabric's shade look different from the photo?

Dye lots vary and screens render colour inconsistently. Always confirm shade against the physical swatch before cutting.
