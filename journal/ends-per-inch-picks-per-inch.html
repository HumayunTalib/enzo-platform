Ends Per Inch and Picks Per Inch: Reading a Fabric's Density Numbers

Cluster: Woven Fabric · Primary keyword: ends per inch picks per inch

Ends per inch (EPI) and picks per inch (PPI) are the actual measured values behind a construction figure like "108 × 76" — the raw density numbers that describe exactly how much yarn is packed into a woven fabric.

What each number counts

  • EPI (ends per inch) — the number of individual warp yarns (ends) crossing one inch of fabric width.
  • PPI (picks per inch) — the number of individual weft insertions (picks) packed into one inch of fabric length.

The convention "108 × 76" means 108 EPI and 76 PPI — see our fuller explainer in reed and pick explained for how these numbers relate to the reed itself.

How to measure it yourself

A pick glass — a small magnifier with a marked measuring window, commonly one inch or half an inch — is placed on the fabric surface. Count the visible ends within the window in the warp direction, and picks in the weft direction, then scale to a full inch if using a half-inch window. This takes under a minute and requires no other equipment.

Why this is the fastest quality check available: a fabric's EPI/PPI count can be verified on the spot against what was quoted. If the numbers don't match the specification sheet, that's an immediate, objective red flag — no lab required.

What the numbers tell you

PatternWhat it suggests
High EPI, high PPIDense, heavy, durable construction
High EPI, low PPIWarp-dominant fabric — often stronger lengthwise
Low EPI, high PPIWeft-dominant fabric — different drape characteristics
Low EPI, low PPIOpen, lightweight construction

EPI/PPI and fabric cost

Both numbers feed directly into the costing formula — see how fabric is priced. Higher counts mean more yarn per metre, which is one of the most direct cost drivers in the entire specification.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need special equipment to check EPI/PPI?

A pick glass makes it fast and precise, but a careful count with a magnifying glass and ruler works too for a rough check.

Can EPI and PPI change after weaving?

No — they're fixed at the weaving stage. Finishing can change fabric width and therefore the effective count per inch slightly, but the underlying yarn count doesn't change after the loom.
