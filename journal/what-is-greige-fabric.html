What Is Greige Fabric? The Starting Point of Every Woven Textile

Cluster: Greige Fabric · Primary keyword: what is greige fabric

Every dyed, printed, or finished fabric started as greige (pronounced "grey") fabric — the cloth as it comes straight off the loom, before any wet processing has touched it.

What greige fabric actually is

Greige fabric has been woven to its final construction — reed, pick, and weave pattern are all already set — but it hasn't been scoured, bleached, dyed, printed, or finished. It typically looks pale, slightly off-white or yellowish depending on the raw fibre, and can feel stiffer and less refined in the hand than the finished cloth it will become, because sizing chemicals applied during weaving to protect the warp are still present.

Why buyers source at the greige stage

  • Cost. Greige is cheaper than finished fabric because dyeing, printing and finishing haven't been paid for yet.
  • Control over the finish. A buyer with their own dyeing relationship can specify exact shades, dye classes, and finishing treatments rather than accepting a mill's standard finish.
  • Flexibility. The same greige base can become many different finished products depending on how it's dyed and finished downstream.
What greige fabric is not: it isn't a lower-quality or defective product — it's an intermediate stage. The construction quality (reed, pick, yarn) is fully determined at the greige stage; dyeing and finishing change appearance and hand, not the underlying weave.

How to evaluate greige fabric

Since colour and finish aren't relevant yet, evaluation focuses entirely on construction: check ends and picks per inch against the quoted specification (see our guide on reed and pick), confirm width, and check for weaving defects — see our guide to common greige fabric defects.

Greige fabric vs finished fabric

See our detailed comparison in greige vs finished fabric: what actually changes — the short version is that weight, hand, and appearance all shift during finishing, sometimes significantly, which is why GSM and shade specifications should always state which stage they refer to.

Frequently asked questions

Is greige fabric the same as raw fabric?

Yes, "greige" and "raw fabric" (or "loomstate fabric") are used interchangeably to describe unfinished woven cloth.

Can greige fabric be sold directly to consumers?

Rarely — greige fabric still carries weaving sizing and hasn't been dyed or finished, so it's almost always sold to buyers who will process it further, not to end consumers.
