Woven Fabric · PILLAR GUIDE

Complete Guide to Woven Fabric Construction

Cluster: Woven Fabric · Primary keyword: woven fabric construction guide

Every woven fabric, regardless of its trade name, is fully described by a small set of construction variables. This guide brings them together in one place.

What makes a fabric "woven"

Two yarn systems interlaced at right angles on a loom — see what is woven fabric? for the full explanation and how it differs from knit construction.

Warp and weft

Warp runs the length of the fabric, set up on the loom first; weft is inserted across it, pick by pick. The two systems are frequently different yarns — see warp vs weft.

Reed and pick: the density numbers

Ends per inch and picks per inch — the two numbers that describe how densely a fabric is woven. See reed and pick explained and reading a fabric's density numbers for how to measure them with a pick glass.

Yarn count: the fineness variable

A separate variable from density — how fine or coarse the yarn itself is. See what yarn count means (remember: higher number = finer yarn) and how yarn count affects fabric weight, hand and cost.

How the variables combine

VariableWhat it controls
Reed × pick (EPI/PPI)Density, durability, weight contribution
Warp/weft yarn countFineness, hand, weight contribution
Fibre contentPerformance characteristics, cost
Weave patternDrape, texture, structural character
Reading a construction figure end to end: "108 × 76, 40/40, 20% Viscose / 80% Polyester" tells you EPI, PPI, warp count, weft count, and composition — a complete, comparable specification, independent of whatever trade name is attached to it.
