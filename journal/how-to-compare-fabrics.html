How to Compare Two Fabrics Side by Side

Cluster: Buying Guides · Primary keyword: how to compare fabrics

Comparing two fabrics by eye alone answers "which do I prefer" but not "which is the better value." Here's a structured way to compare that actually accounts for what's different.

Step 1: Get both specifications on the same basis

Confirm construction (reed × pick — see reed and pick explained), GSM (and whether greige or finished — see greige vs finished fabric), width, and composition for both. You can't fairly compare price until you know you're comparing the same things.

Step 2: Check hand and drape directly

Use the hands-on checks from our fabric-identification checklist — crush and release for crease recovery, hold to light for opacity and weave openness, drape over your hand to feel fall.

Step 3: Normalise cost for your actual use

If you're comparing for garment production, calculate cost per garment using each fabric's width and your pattern's consumption — not just rate per metre. See GSM and width: how they change cost per metre for a full worked comparison showing how this can flip which fabric is actually cheaper.

Step 4: Match properties to your actual requirement

A fabric that wins on every measurable property still isn't the better choice if those properties don't matter for your use case — see our framework in quality vs price.

A simple comparison table works better than memory. Write down construction, GSM, width, composition, and price for both fabrics side by side before deciding — differences that seem obvious in person are easy to lose track of once you're comparing more than two options.
