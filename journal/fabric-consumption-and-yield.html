Fabric Consumption and Yield: Getting Garment Costing Right

Cluster: Garment Manufacturing · Primary keyword: fabric consumption and yield

Two buyers can pay the same rate per metre for identical fabric and end up with meaningfully different costs per garment, because consumption and yield — not the quoted rate — determine the real cost.

Consumption: how much fabric one garment actually uses

Consumption is driven by the pattern (marker), fabric width, and how efficiently pattern pieces can be laid out on the cloth. A garment cut from a wider fabric generally consumes fewer linear metres than the same garment cut from a narrower one — see our piece on how GSM and width change cost per metre.

Yield: how many garments a given quantity of fabric produces

Yield (garments per 100m) = 100 ÷ Consumption per garment (metres)

A construction that consumes 2.0m per garment yields 50 garments per 100 metres; one that consumes 2.2m yields roughly 45. That difference compounds fast across a production order.

What affects consumption beyond width

  • Pattern complexity. More pattern pieces, or an irregular pattern shape, generally increases wastage between pieces on the marker.
  • Directional fabric. A one-way nap, print, or stripe restricts how pieces can be laid out, reducing marker efficiency.
  • Fabric defects. Every defect that has to be cut around effectively increases the fabric needed — see our note on this in reducing fabric wastage in bulk production.
Why this matters for supplier comparison: the true cost driver for a bulk buyer is cost per garment, not cost per metre — see our worked comparison in GSM and width showing how a higher rate-per-metre fabric can still be the cheaper option once consumption is factored in.

A practical marker-efficiency check

Before committing to a bulk order, ask your pattern or production team to run a test marker at the actual fabric width being quoted — not an assumed standard width. Small width differences between suppliers can shift consumption meaningfully across a full production run.

Frequently asked questions

Is a wider fabric always more efficient?

Generally yes for consumption, but confirm with an actual marker test rather than assuming — pattern shape and directional fabric can offset some of the width advantage.

How much wastage is normal in garment cutting?

It varies significantly by garment type and pattern complexity — there's no single industry-standard figure, which is exactly why a real marker test matters more than a rule of thumb.
