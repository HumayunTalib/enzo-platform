In Pakistan’s cloth trade the Eid season starts on the shop floor weeks before Ramazan, and at the mill months before that. Retailers aim to have their stock in before 15 Shaban. That means wholesalers must deliver earlier still, and the fabric itself has to be booked, woven and finished before then. This guide sets out the seasonal calendar, gives the expected dates for 2027, and shows how to plan backwards from the shop shelf to the loom.

The trade calendar around Ramazan and Eid

Eid ul Fitr is the biggest buying moment for men’s unstitched fabric. Families buy new suits for Eid, and many buy for gifting during Ramazan. Retailers want the full range on display before the fasting month begins, so that customers can shop early and tailors have time to stitch.

The trade works to a clear marker. Reporting on Karachi’s wholesale Kapra Market, Dawn noted that retailers were expected to “complete their stock piling of various fabrics and readymade suits before the 15th of Shaban”, ahead of Ramazan (Dawn, 21 February 2021). The same report said that around 95 per cent of the fabric arriving in that market came from Faisalabad, with some from Gujranwala and Lahore, and that most of it arrived already finished and dyed. In other words, the fabric that sells in Ramazan has usually been woven and processed somewhere else, well before the selling season begins.

Eid ul Adha comes about two months after Eid ul Fitr and brings a second, usually smaller, peak for men’s clothing. Summer weights then take over. This article focuses on the Eid ul Fitr cycle because it sets the pace for the year.

Expected dates for 2027

Islamic dates follow the lunar calendar and are confirmed only after moon sighting, so the Gregorian dates below are estimates. They can move by a day, and Pakistan’s dates can differ by a day from those announced in the Gulf.

The working deadline for the cloth trade is therefore the third or fourth week of January 2027. Stock that reaches a retailer after that still sells, but it arrives when shelves are already full and buying decisions have been made.

Planning backwards from the shelf

The safest way to plan is to start from the date the fabric must be on the shelf and work back through each stage. Every stage has its own time, and the times differ between suppliers, constructions, shades and quantities. Do not borrow someone else’s lead times. Ask each supplier for theirs, in writing, and add a margin.

Add up the stage times your suppliers give you, then add a buffer for re-dyeing, transport delays and approvals. The date you get is your last safe booking date. For most of the trade it falls well before the end of the Gregorian year.

What happens at each stage

Booking. Agree the specification in writing: construction, blend, width, GSM, shades and quantity. A clear specification avoids a second round of sampling later. Our list of questions before a bulk fabric order is a useful starting point.

Weaving. Greige is woven to the agreed construction. Yarn availability and loom capacity both tighten as the season approaches, because many buyers are booking at once. See how greige fabric is manufactured.

Finishing. The greige is dyed and finished. Shade matching is the step most likely to need a second attempt, so approve lab dips or strike-offs early. The trade-offs between buying at each stage are covered in greige vs finished fabric.

Inspection and dispatch. Check metres, faults, width and shade before the cloth leaves. Problems found after it reaches the shop cost far more time to put right. Use our fabric inspection checklist.

Shelf. The wholesaler cuts or packs suit lengths and delivers to shops in time for the 15 Shaban stocking deadline.

Where the plan usually slips

  • Late specification. Changing the construction or shade after booking resets the clock.
  • Shade approval. Waiting for a buyer’s approval on each shade can hold up a whole lot.
  • Peak congestion. Processors and transport get busier as Ramazan approaches.
  • Calendar drift. Ramazan moves about 10 to 11 days earlier each Gregorian year, so last year’s dates are not this year’s. In 2027 the deadline is in January, not February.

A booking checklist

  1. Note the expected 15 Shaban date and treat it as the shelf deadline.
  2. Get written stage times from each supplier.
  3. Work back and add a buffer to find your last booking date.
  4. Fix the written specification and approve samples before that date.
  5. Agree how and when the cloth will be inspected before dispatch.

Frequently asked questions

When do retailers stock up for Ramazan and Eid in Pakistan?

Retailers aim to complete stocking before 15 Shaban, about two weeks before Ramazan begins, according to Dawn’s reporting on Karachi’s cloth market.

When is 15 Shaban in 2027?

It is expected around 23–24 January 2027, subject to moon sighting.

When is Ramazan 2027 expected to start?

Around 8 February 2027, with Eid ul Fitr expected around 9 or 10 March 2027. Both depend on moon sighting.

How early should a wholesaler book fabric for Eid?

Early enough that weaving, finishing, inspection and transport are all complete before 15 Shaban, plus a buffer. Ask each supplier for its stage times in writing and work backwards.

Why do Eid dates change every year?

The Islamic calendar is lunar, so Ramazan and Eid move about 10 to 11 days earlier each Gregorian year.

Conclusion

The trade’s real Eid deadline is 15 Shaban, not Eid itself. For 2027 that falls in late January. Work backwards from it, get every supplier’s stage times in writing, and book while there is still room to fix a shade or a fault.
