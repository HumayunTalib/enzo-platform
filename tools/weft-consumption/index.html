Definition: Weft consumptionWeft consumption is the weight of weft yarn needed to weave one metre of fabric. It depends on picks per inch, the width each pick must cross, the yarn count and the loss at the selvedge.

Formula

Picks per metre = PPI × 39.3701
Weft length per metre of fabric = picks per metre × reed space (m)
Weft weight (g/m) = weft length (m) × (590.541 ÷ Ne) ÷ 1000 × (1 + waste)

Where it comes from

Each pick crosses the full reed space once. A metre of fabric has PPI × 39.3701 picks, so the weft length is picks × pick length; multiplying by tex ÷ 1000 converts length to grams.

Assumptions and limits

  • Count in Ne. Pick length = reed space. Waste is your own figure.
  • PPI is the loom pick density; finished PPI is usually higher after width shrinkage.
  • If you enter finished PPI with reed space, the weft figure is slightly overstated; use loom PPI for loom planning.

Frequently asked

How is weft consumption calculated?

Picks per metre × reed space in metres gives yarn length; multiply by tex ÷ 1000. 80 PPI, 58 in, Ne 36 gives about 76 g per metre.

Why does weft use depend on reed space?

Every pick travels across the whole reed width, so wider reed means more yarn per pick.
