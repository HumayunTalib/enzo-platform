Definition: GSMGSM (grams per square metre) is the mass of one square metre of fabric. For woven fabric it can be calculated from ends per inch, picks per inch, yarn counts and crimp, or measured by weighing a cut sample of known area.

Formula

GSM = 23.25 × [ EPI × (1 + warp crimp) ÷ Ne(warp) + PPI × (1 + weft crimp) ÷ Ne(weft) ]
where 23.25 = 39.3701 in/m × 0.590541 (tex = 590.541 ÷ Ne) ÷ 1000

Where it comes from

One metre of fabric width holds EPI × 39.3701 warp ends. Each end needs 1 m × (1 + crimp) of yarn, and a yarn of count Ne weighs 590.541 ÷ Ne grams per 1,000 m. Adding the weft the same way gives the formula above.

Cut-sample method

GSM = sample mass (g) ÷ sample area (m²)
Area (circle) = π × (d ÷ 2)² · Area (rectangle) = width × length · 1 cm² = 0.0001 m²

Assumptions and limits

  • Count is English cotton count (Ne); for other systems convert first with the yarn count converter.
  • Crimp % = (yarn length − fabric length) ÷ fabric length × 100, measured on a sample.
  • EPI and PPI are measured on the finished or greige fabric you are weighing; the result matches that state.
  • Theoretical GSM ignores sizing, finishing chemicals and moisture. Always confirm against a cut-sample weight.
  • If you only know the fabric on the loom, remember that finished width and density differ from reed width and loom density.

Frequently asked

How do I calculate GSM from EPI and PPI?

Multiply (EPI ÷ warp Ne + PPI ÷ weft Ne) by 23.25. With 100 EPI, 80 PPI and Ne 36 for both, that is 180 ÷ 36 × 23.25 = 116.3 GSM before crimp.

Why is real GSM higher than the calculated value?

Crimp adds yarn length, and sizing, finishing and moisture add mass. Enter crimp from a sample to close part of the gap.
