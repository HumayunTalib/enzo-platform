Definition: Cover factorCover factor describes how closely threads are packed relative to their thickness. It lets constructions with different counts and densities be compared on a single scale.

Formula

Kw = EPI ÷ √Ne(warp) Kf = PPI ÷ √Ne(weft)
K (fabric) = Kw + Kf − (Kw × Kf ÷ 28)
Average float length = repeat size ÷ number of up/down changes per thread in the repeat

Where it comes from

Yarn diameter is proportional to 1 ÷ √count, so EPI ÷ √Ne tracks how much of the cloth width each thread system occupies. The −Kw·Kf ÷ 28 term corrects for the overlap of the two thread systems.

Assumptions and limits

  • Ne basis. The 10–22 (open) and 22–35 (close) ranges are quoted for plain fabrics; they are a reference scale, not a quality grade.
  • Float length is derived from the weave repeat: plain 1.0, 2/1 twill 1.5, 2/2 and 3/1 twill 2.0, 5-end satin 2.5 threads.
  • Longer floats allow more threads to be packed than in plain weave, but need more care against snagging; cover factor alone does not predict hand or durability.
  • Compare constructions only on the same weave and fibre type.

Frequently asked

What is cover factor?

A number combining thread density and yarn fineness: EPI ÷ √Ne for warp, PPI ÷ √Ne for weft, combined with a correction term.

What is a good cover factor?

It depends on end use. For plain fabrics the reference ranges are 10–22 (open) and 22–35 (close); tight cloth is not automatically better for every use.
