Definition: Warp consumptionWarp consumption is the weight of warp yarn needed to weave one metre of fabric. It depends on the number of ends across the reed, the yarn count, the crimp and the loss in preparation.

Formula

Total ends = EPI × reed space (in) + selvedge ends
Yarn length per metre of fabric = total ends × (1 + crimp)
Warp weight (g/m) = yarn length (m) × (590.541 ÷ Ne) ÷ 1000 × (1 + waste)

Where it comes from

Each warp end runs the full length of the fabric. If the fabric is 1 m long and crimp is c, each end consumes 1 + c metres of yarn. Multiply by the number of ends and by the yarn mass per metre (tex ÷ 1000).

Worked example

Assumptions and limits

  • Count in Ne; weight is for the greige beam width (reed space).
  • Crimp and waste are inputs you supply; no default is assumed.
  • Reed space is wider than finished fabric width because fabric shrinks in width after weaving and finishing.
  • Compare with the GSM calculator: warp weight per metre ÷ finished width (m) gives the warp share of GSM when crimp and width basis match.

Frequently asked

How do I calculate warp weight per metre?

Total ends × (1 + crimp) × (590.541 ÷ Ne) ÷ 1000. For 5,800 ends of Ne 36 with no crimp that is about 95.1 g per metre.

What is reed space?

The width of warp threads across the reed on the loom, before the fabric shrinks in width.
