Definition: Yarn countYarn count states how fine a yarn is. Indirect systems (Ne, Nm, NeK) give length per unit mass, so a higher number is finer. Direct systems (tex, denier) give mass per unit length, so a higher number is coarser.

Formula

tex = 590.541 ÷ Ne · tex = 1000 ÷ Nm · tex = denier ÷ 9 · tex = 885.81 ÷ NeK
Ply (indirect systems Ne, Nm, NeK): resultant count = single count ÷ number of plies
Ply (direct systems tex, denier): resultant = single value × number of plies

Where it comes from

Ne counts 840-yard hanks per pound, NeK counts 560-yard hanks per pound, Nm counts 1,000 m per kg, tex is grams per 1,000 m and denier grams per 9,000 m. Converting any system to tex first and then to the target keeps every conversion consistent.

Assumptions and limits

  • 36/1 means one single yarn of Ne 36. 36/2 means two Ne 36 yarns plied, resultant Ne 18.
  • Counts are nominal values; real yarn varies along its length.
  • A 36/2 yarn is twice as heavy per metre as 36/1 and is not a finer yarn.
  • Round trips can differ in the last digit because of rounding.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between 36/1 and 36/2?

36/1 is a single yarn of Ne 36 (16.4 tex). 36/2 is two Ne 36 yarns plied together: resultant Ne 18, 32.8 tex.

How do I convert Ne to tex?

Divide 590.541 by the Ne count: Ne 30 = 19.68 tex.
