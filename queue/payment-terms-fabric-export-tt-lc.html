Payment Terms for Fabric Export Orders: T/T, Letter of Credit, Documentary Collection and Open Account

By ENZO Textiles · Published {{PUB_DATE_H}} · 4 min read

Payment terms are the part of a fabric order where buyer and seller are most exposed. Pay too early and you carry the risk that goods never arrive; ship too early and the seller carries the risk of not being paid. International trade has a small set of standard payment methods, each placing risk differently. This guide explains them in plain terms so you can agree a structure that fits the size of the order and the history between the parties. It is general information, not financial or legal advice.

The risk spectrum

The main methods sit on a spectrum from safest for the exporter to safest for the importer: cash in advance, letter of credit, documentary collection and open account (Xflow). Telegraphic transfer (T/T) is a different layer: it is the bank-to-bank wire used to execute a payment, not a risk structure on its own, so “T/T” can describe an advance payment, a balance payment or an open-account settlement.

Letters of credit and UCP 600

An L/C is a bank undertaking, governed by the ICC’s Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600), which has been in force since 1 July 2007 (DHL). Payment depends on documents matching the credit’s terms, so document preparation has to be exact. L/Cs add bank fees and administrative strictness compared with a simple wire (Taxtmi). For a first, high-value order between parties who have not traded before, many buyers and sellers consider an L/C or a staged payment.

Staged payments

Between the extremes, a common structure is a part payment by T/T at order confirmation and the balance before shipment or against documents. Where the balance is paid before the goods are released, the seller is protected against non-payment and the buyer can ask for inspection and test reports first (see fabric quality tests and the 4-point inspection system). Pre-shipment inspection is a natural trigger for the balance. The exact percentages are a commercial negotiation; there is no universal split.

How to choose

  • Order size and value. Larger orders justify more security (L/C or staged payments).
  • Relationship. Repeat partners may use open account with credit insurance; new partners usually need more protection.
  • Documents and timing. L/Cs need exact documents, so build time for them into the schedule.
  • Cost. Compare bank fees with the risk you are covering.
  • Delivery term. Payment trigger points often relate to the Incoterm; see Incoterms for fabric shipments.

Protect yourself as a buyer

  1. Pay only to the company account named on the contract, never to a personal account.
  2. Agree inspection and testing before the balance is released.
  3. Define what happens to defective goods.
  4. Keep every term in writing: price, quantity, specification, Incoterm, payment schedule.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between T/T and L/C?

T/T is a bank wire that can carry any payment. An L/C is a bank’s undertaking to pay against compliant documents, governed by UCP 600.

Which payment method is safest for a buyer?

For the buyer, open account is safest and cash in advance riskiest; an L/C and staged payments with pre-shipment inspection sit between.

What is open account?

The seller ships first and the buyer pays later, commonly 30–90 days. It carries the highest risk for the exporter.

Is a letter of credit worth the cost?

It adds fees and strict documentation but gives both sides assurance. It suits larger first orders; smaller or repeat orders often use staged T/T.

Should I pay 100% in advance?

That puts all the risk on the buyer. For a first order, consider staged payments with inspection before the balance.

Conclusion

Match the payment structure to the order size and the relationship, tie the balance to inspection, and put every term in writing. Payment terms are negotiated per order, so state what you need in your enquiry.
