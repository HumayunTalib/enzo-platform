80 × 72 Greige Fabric Explained: Reading the Construction Before You Buy

By ENZO Textiles · Published {{PUB_DATE_H}} · 6 min read

Search for greige fabric in Pakistan and you will meet the same shorthand again and again: “80 × 72”, often followed by “40/40”. To a mill these numbers are a full construction. To a new buyer they can look like a code. This guide reads 80 × 72 one number at a time, shows what it tells you and what it leaves out, and lists what to confirm in writing before you place a greige order.

Reading 80 × 72

The first number is the reed: warp ends per inch, the threads running along the length of the cloth. The second is the pick: weft threads per inch, the threads running across it. So 80 × 72 means a one-inch square holds 80 warp threads and 72 weft threads. Our guides to reed and pick and ends per inch and picks per inch explain how both are counted with a pick glass. Standard test methods for counting ends and picks exist, for example ASTM D3775.

A little arithmetic helps you compare constructions:

  • Total threads per square inch: 80 + 72 = 152. A 112 × 84 construction has 112 + 84 = 196, so it packs about 29% more threads into the same square inch.
  • Balance: 80 ÷ 72 is about 1.11, close to square. The warp has only slightly more threads than the weft.
  • Warp first: by trade convention the reed is quoted before the pick. If a quote reads “72 × 80”, ask which direction is which.

A lower thread density is not a fault. It is a different cloth, with a different handle and different end use. Compare it only with constructions meant for the same purpose.

What 40/40 adds

The pair 40/40 gives the yarn count of the warp and then the weft. Here both directions use the same count. Reed × pick tells you how many threads sit in an inch; the yarn count tells you how thick each thread is. Together they decide how much cloth you get and how it feels. Our note on what yarn count means covers the numbering systems, and the yarn count and fabric weight guide shows how count feeds into weight.

Ask which count system is being used, and whether a count is quoted for a single yarn or a plied one. Two quotes that both say “40/40” are only comparable if they use the same system.

What the numbers do not tell you

Reed × pick and count are the core of a greige specification, but they are not the whole of it. Four things are still open until the mill states them:

  • Width. It sets how much usable cloth each metre gives. At ENZO the width is usually 64 inches; confirm it for your quality. See fabric width and garment consumption.
  • GSM. Weight varies by quality. Work it out from your own construction with the GSM from EPI and PPI guide, then confirm it against a weighed sample.
  • Greige versus finished count. Dyeing and finishing can move the count. A construction quoted on greige will not be identical after processing.
  • Appearance and defects. Numbers do not show loom faults. Read greige fabric defects and agree an inspection method first.

Frequently asked questions

What does 80 × 72 mean in fabric?

It is the reed × pick: 80 warp ends and 72 weft picks per inch of cloth.

Does 80 × 72 tell me the GSM?

No. GSM also depends on yarn count and finishing. Ask for it in writing.

Conclusion

80 × 72 is a density: 152 threads per square inch, nearly square in balance. It becomes a buying specification only when the yarn count, width, GSM, quantity and lead time are stated beside it. Write them all down before you commit.
