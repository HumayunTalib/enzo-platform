How to Calculate Woven Fabric GSM from EPI, PPI and Yarn Count

By ENZO Textiles · Published 19 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article
  1. Deriving the formula
  2. Worked example
  3. The cut-sample method
  4. Why calculated and measured values differ

GSM (grams per square metre) is the number buyers quote most often and the one most often disputed. For woven fabric you can calculate it from the construction or measure it from a sample. This article derives the formula, works an example and explains why the two methods disagree.

DefinitionGSM is the mass of one square metre of fabric in grams. Woven-fabric GSM is the sum of the warp yarn and the weft yarn in that square metre, plus anything added in finishing.

Deriving the formula

Start with one square metre: 1 m wide and 1 m long.

  1. The width holds EPI × 39.3701 warp ends (there are 39.3701 inches in a metre).
  2. Each end runs the full 1 m of fabric length but, because it weaves over and under the picks, it uses 1 + crimp metres of yarn.
  3. A yarn of count tex weighs tex ÷ 1000 grams per metre, and tex = 590.541 ÷ Ne.

Warp weight = EPI × 39.3701 × (1 + crimpw) × 590.541 ÷ New ÷ 1000. Collecting constants, 39.3701 × 590.541 ÷ 1000 = 23.25. The weft follows the same logic with PPI.

GSM = 23.25 × [ EPI × (1 + crimpw) ÷ Newarp + PPI × (1 + crimpf) ÷ Neweft ]

Worked example

A construction of 100 ends × 80 picks per inch with Ne 36 warp and Ne 36 weft, ignoring crimp:

Warp = 23.25 × 100 ÷ 36 = 64.6 g/m²
Weft = 23.25 × 80 ÷ 36 = 51.7 g/m²
GSM = 116.2

If the same fabric has illustrative crimps of 8% in the warp and 5% in the weft, the figure becomes 124 GSM. Crimp is measured on a sample; the 8% and 5% here are examples only.

The cut-sample method

Cut a sample of known area, weigh it and scale to one square metre. A 100 cm² circle (diameter 11.28 cm) weighs one-hundredth of a square metre, so GSM = mass in grams × 100. A 5 cm × 20 cm rectangle is also 100 cm². Take several samples across the width and along the length and average them.

Why calculated and measured values differ

  • Crimp. Ignoring it understates weight; the figure depends on weave, density and tension.
  • Sizing and finishing. Size on the warp and finishing chemicals add mass the formula does not include.
  • Moisture. Fibres hold different amounts of water, so the same fabric weighs differently at different humidity.
  • Width basis. EPI on the loom and EPI on the finished fabric are different numbers because the fabric shrinks in width. Use the basis that matches the state you are weighing.

Treat a calculated GSM as a planning figure and the weighed sample as the specification. State which one you mean and give a tolerance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the GSM formula for woven fabric?

GSM = 23.25 × [EPI × (1 + warp crimp) ÷ Ne warp + PPI × (1 + weft crimp) ÷ Ne weft], where crimp is a fraction.

How do I measure GSM without a formula?

Cut a sample of known area (a 100 cm² circle is common), weigh it in grams and multiply by 100.

Why does my measured GSM differ from the calculated value?

Crimp, sizing, finishing chemicals and moisture all change the real weight; the calculation covers only the yarn in the cloth.
