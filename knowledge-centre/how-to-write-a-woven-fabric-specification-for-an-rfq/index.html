How to Write a Woven Fabric Specification for an RFQ

By ENZO Textiles · Published 19 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article
  1. The fields
  2. Getting the numbers right
  3. Partial specifications are fine
  4. Checklist before you send

A request for quotation that leaves out a field gets a quotation that makes an assumption. When the assumption is wrong, the price changes after the order. A written specification, even a partial one, moves those decisions to the beginning.

DefinitionA woven fabric specification is the written description of a fabric precise enough that two suppliers could make the same cloth from it: fibre, yarn, construction, size, weight, finish, colour and acceptance criteria.

The fields

FieldWhat to writeCommon mistake
CompositionFibre content and ratio, e.g. polyester/viscose with the percentagesGiving the blend name only
Yarn countWarp and weft count with system, ply and spinning method if it matters (e.g. Ne 36/1)A bare number with no system
ConstructionEPI × PPI, and whether measured on greige or finished clothMixing loom and finished density
WeavePlain, twill type, satin or a drawing/referenceLeaving it implied
WidthWidth and basis (selvedge to selvedge or usable)Not stating the basis
WeightGSM target with tolerance and basis (greige or finished)A single number with no tolerance
FinishRequired finishing steps and performance targetsOmitting finishing
ColourGreige, or shade reference and approval method (swatch, lab dip)Describing colour by name only
Quantity and packingTotal metres, roll length range, packing and labellingQuantity in yards in one place, metres in another
InspectionMethod, sample size and acceptance limit (see the pre-shipment inspection article)No acceptance criteria
DeliveryDestination, Incoterm and target dateLeaving the destination until after the quote

Getting the numbers right

  • Convert counts with the yarn count converter so the quote and your internal spec use the same system.
  • Check that GSM, EPI, PPI and counts agree with the GSM calculator. A target GSM that does not match the construction is a common source of disputes.
  • Use the unit converter for metres, yards and oz/yd².

Partial specifications are fine

You do not need every field to start. State what you know and mark the rest "open". ENZO reviews the specification before quoting and replies with what is missing. The RFQ form carries the key fields, and a printable specification sheet template collects them all.

Checklist before you send

  • Composition with percentages
  • Warp and weft count, with system and ply
  • EPI × PPI and the basis
  • Width and basis
  • GSM target and tolerance
  • Greige or dyed; shade reference
  • Quantity in metres
  • Inspection method and acceptance limit
  • Destination and target date

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum information for a fabric RFQ?

Composition, construction or reference sample, width, quantity and whether the fabric is greige or dyed. A supplier can ask for the rest.

Should GSM be a single number?

State a target and a tolerance, and say whether it applies to greige or finished fabric.

Can I send a sample instead of a specification?

A reference sample is useful alongside the specification, and many details can be read from it, but written figures remove ambiguity.
