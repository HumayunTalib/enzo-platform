Cover Factor and Fabric Balance: Comparing Woven Constructions on One Scale

By ENZO Textiles · Published 19 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article
  1. The formulas
  2. Reading the range
  3. Comparing two constructions
  4. Balance and weave

Two fabrics with different yarn counts and different EPI × PPI cannot be compared by density alone. Cover factor puts them on one scale. This article explains the calculation, the reference ranges and how the weave changes what is possible.

DefinitionCover factor measures how much of a fabric's area is covered by its threads, based on thread density and yarn fineness. Warp cover factor, weft cover factor and a combined fabric cover factor are all in use.

The formulas

Yarn diameter varies with 1 ÷ √count, so EPI ÷ √Ne tracks the share of the width each thread system fills. The combined figure corrects for overlap between the two systems (formulas as given by Textile Learner):

Kw = EPI ÷ √Newarp
Kf = PPI ÷ √Neweft
K = Kw + Kf − (Kw × Kf ÷ 28)

For 100 × 80 at Ne 36 / Ne 36: Kw = 16.67, Kf = 13.33, K = 22.06.

Reading the range

For plain fabrics the same source puts open-set fabrics at about 10 to 22 and close-set fabrics at about 22 to 35. These are reference bands, not a grade. A fabric at the edge of a band is simply near the boundary.

Balance and weave

Balance is the EPI : PPI ratio. Many suiting constructions have more ends than picks, so the ratio is above 1. A strongly unbalanced cloth behaves differently in the two directions.

The weave sets how threads interlace. The average float length is the weave repeat divided by the number of interlacing changes: plain weave 1.0; 2/1 twill 1.5; 2/2 and 3/1 twill 2.0; five-end satin 2.5. Fewer interlacings leave more room to pack threads, which is why twill and satin constructions can reach higher densities than plain weave. Longer floats also snag more easily, so density is not the only factor in choosing a weave.

Frequently asked questions

What is cover factor?

A number combining thread density and fineness: EPI ÷ √Ne for the warp, PPI ÷ √Ne for the weft, combined into one fabric figure.

What cover factor range is open or close set?

For plain fabrics the source quoted here gives roughly 10–22 for open set and 22–35 for close set.

Does a higher cover factor mean better fabric?

No. It means a tighter build, which suits some uses and not others.
