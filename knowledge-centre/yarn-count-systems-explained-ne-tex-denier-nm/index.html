Yarn Count Systems Explained: Ne, Nm, Tex, Denier and What 36/1 vs 36/2 Means

By ENZO Textiles · Published 19 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article
  1. Direct and indirect systems
  2. Conversion formulas
  3. Reading 36/1 and 36/2
  4. What this means for a specification

Every woven-fabric specification starts with yarn counts, and every supplier quotes them in a different system. A buyer who cannot convert between Ne, Nm, tex and denier cannot compare quotations, check a construction or work out a fabric weight. This article sets out the systems, the formulas that connect them and how to read a ply notation such as 36/1 or 36/2.

DefinitionYarn count is a measure of yarn fineness expressed as length per unit mass (indirect systems: Ne, Nm, NeK) or mass per unit length (direct systems: tex, denier).

Direct and indirect systems

In an indirect system the count is the length of yarn that weighs one unit of mass, so a higher number means a finer yarn. Ne (English cotton count), Nm (metric count) and the worsted count NeK are indirect. In a direct system the count is the mass of a fixed length, so a higher number means a coarser yarn. Tex and denier are direct.

Conversion formulas

Because every system can be expressed through tex, conversions are one step each way. The constants follow from the unit definitions (1 lb = 453.59 g; 840 yd = 768.1 m). They match the values published by Textile School (Ne = 590.5 ÷ tex; Nm = Ne × 1.693).

tex = 590.541 ÷ Ne
tex = 1000 ÷ Nm
tex = denier ÷ 9
tex = 885.81 ÷ NeK
Nm = 1.693 × Ne

Reading 36/1 and 36/2

In the notation 36/2 the first number is the count of each single yarn and the second is the number of plies twisted together. 36/1 is therefore a single Ne 36 yarn. 36/2 is two Ne 36 yarns plied.

For indirect systems the resultant count is the single count divided by the number of plies; for direct systems the value is multiplied by the number of plies. Two Ne 36 yarns have a resultant count of Ne 18, which is 32.8 tex against 16.4 tex for a single Ne 36 yarn. This is a nominal figure: twisting the plies together takes up a little length, so measured counts differ slightly.

What this means for a specification

  • State the system. Write "Ne 36" or "36/1 Ne", not just "36". A bare number is ambiguous because Ne 36 and Nm 36 are very different yarns.
  • State warp and weft separately. They are often different. The GSM calculator takes both.
  • State single or plied. A plied yarn changes the fabric weight and handle.
  • Finer yarn, lighter cloth. At the same ends and picks per inch, a finer yarn gives a lighter fabric. The weight formula is in How to calculate woven fabric GSM.

Counts such as 36/1, 37/1 and 26/1 PV are among those ENZO weaves; the count for any order is confirmed in the quotation.

Frequently asked questions

What does 36/1 mean in yarn count?

It is a single yarn (one ply) with an English cotton count of Ne 36. The number before the slash is the count of each single yarn; the number after is the number of plies.

What is the difference between 36/1 and 36/2?

36/2 is two Ne 36 yarns plied together. Its resultant count is about Ne 18 and it is twice as heavy per metre as 36/1. It is not a finer yarn.

How do I convert Ne to tex?

Divide 590.541 by the Ne count. Ne 30 is 19.68 tex.
