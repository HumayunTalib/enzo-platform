Bulk Fabric Pre-Shipment Inspection: What an Independent Final Check Should Cover

By ENZO Textiles · Published 19 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article
  1. What it covers
  2. How the four-point system scores defects
  3. What to agree before the order
  4. ENZO's inspection role

Most fabric disputes are decided at the dock, not the loom. A pre-shipment inspection is the check made on the finished fabric before it is released, and the agreement on how it will be done belongs in the RFQ, not after the problem.

DefinitionA pre-shipment fabric inspection examines a defined sample of finished rolls for defects, width, weight, shade continuity and packing against the agreed specification and acceptance limit before the goods are shipped.

What it covers

  • Visual defects found on an inspection frame, scored by a system such as the four-point system.
  • Width measured across several points.
  • Weight (GSM) from cut samples; see how to calculate and measure GSM.
  • Shade continuity within a roll and between rolls of the same lot.
  • Roll length, packing and labelling against the order.

How the four-point system scores defects

Each visible defect is given 1 to 4 penalty points by size. The V-Trust summary of ASTM D5430 gives:

Defect lengthPenalty points
3 inches or less1
Over 3 to 6 inches2
Over 6 to 9 inches3
Over 9 inches4

A single flaw is capped at 4 points, and per that source only major defects are penalised, not minor ones. The total is converted to a score per 100 square yards:

Points per 100 yd² = total points × 3,600 ÷ (yards inspected × fabric width in inches)

The source notes that some factories treat up to 40 points per 100 square yards as acceptable, and that buyers set their own standards. The system can be used with "criteria and tolerance agreed previously between buyers and sellers". So the limit is a commercial term, not a fixed rule.

What to agree before the order

ItemDecide
MethodFour-point system or another named method
Acceptance limitPoints per 100 yd², or other criteria
Sample sizeHow many rolls, and how much of each is inspected
Who inspectsSupplier, buyer, or a third party
RemedyWhat happens if a lot fails: re-inspection, credit, replacement

ENZO's inspection role

ENZO carries out an independent final quality inspection before dispatch. The inspector is separate from loom operations and is compensated from the unit's profit (see the manufacturing page). If your contract requires a specific method or a third-party inspector, put it in the RFQ and the quotation will reflect it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the four-point system?

A method of scoring visible fabric defects with 1 to 4 penalty points according to their size, then comparing the total with an agreed limit per 100 square yards.

What is an acceptable score?

There is no universal limit. Some factories treat up to 40 points per 100 square yards as acceptable, but buyers set their own standards.

Who should carry out the inspection?

The party named in the contract. ENZO carries out an independent final inspection before dispatch; a buyer can also appoint its own inspector.
